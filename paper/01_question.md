# 1. Question

Have heat and drought extremes become more frequent or more intense in Spain,
France, Germany and Sweden, and how much of that change can be described by a
shift in the mean? We compare 1961-1990 with 1996-2025 using country-level
ERA5-Land data from the Copernicus Interactive Climate Atlas. Our central
question is whether the recent distribution resembles the early distribution
moved to a new level, or whether its extreme tail has moved further than the
mean.

The distinction matters for risk assessment. A fixed historical threshold can
be crossed much more often after the mean changes, even if variability around
the mean stays constant. More exceedances therefore do not, by themselves,
establish that a tail has become heavier. For water managers, agricultural
producers and planners, both the level of the climate variable and the
distribution around that level matter. Our data measure climate conditions;
they do not measure economic losses or identify the benefits of an adaptation
policy.

Three references frame the question. IPCC AR6 Chapter 11 documents changes in
the frequency and intensity of temperature extremes and assesses their human
contribution using evidence beyond simple historical comparisons (Seneviratne
et al., 2021, Sections 11.2-11.3). Hansen, Sato and Ruedy (2012) show a shift
toward hotter seasonal temperature anomalies and a larger occurrence of
exceptionally hot summers. Rhines and Huybers (2013) question the inference of
increased variability from those distributions: normalisation, trends and
changing station coverage can produce apparent widening. Their result makes
the empirical task clear. We must compare tail changes with a shift benchmark,
rather than infer a change in shape from an increase in extremes alone. These
papers concern seasonal temperatures and different spatial aggregations; our
analysis is not a direct replication.

We use two complementary indices: monthly TXx for June-August, measuring the
country average of local monthly temperature maxima, and September SPEI-6,
summarising April-September climatic water balance. The four countries span
southern to northern Europe, allowing us to compare contrasting climates
within one dataset. TXx values are expressed relative to each country's
early-period mean for the same calendar month. We examine its upper tail at
P75, P90, P95 and P99. For SPEI-6, lower values mean drier conditions, so the
corresponding thresholds are P25, P10, P5 and P1.

An answer requires three comparisons: the change in the mean and spread; the
change in tail percentiles relative to the mean; and the frequency of crossing
fixed early-period thresholds. Time trends and quantile regressions provide a
further check on whether the centre and tails move at different rates. The
results describe these four country-level series over the observed record.
They do not attribute individual events to greenhouse gas emissions or predict
future extremes.
